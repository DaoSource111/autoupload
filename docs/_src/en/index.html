The app

Put each finished video in its channel's queue folder with a short details file. Preview dates shows the publishing slot each one will get; Upload asks you to confirm, then sends them. You can try every button without a Google account: start the app with --demo.

Why it is different

  • Native, written in Rust. One small program per system, with nothing else to install: no Python, no Node, no browser engine inside.
  • Local-first. No AutoUpload server or account. Your files, sign-in and history stay on your computer, and it uses your own Google project and quota.
  • Built for AI agents. Claude Code, Codex and other agents can queue your videos with titles, descriptions and tags, and preview the dates. They upload only if you allow it, and the app shows everything they did.
  • Safe by default. Nothing is uploaded without your confirmation, nothing goes public at once, and the same file is never uploaded twice.

Let an AI agent do the busy work

AutoUpload works as an MCP server, the standard way AI agents use tools. Tell your agent “queue every video in my exports folder with a good title and tags, then preview the dates”, and watch it happen in the Activity tab. How to connect an agent.

What AutoUpload does

  • Uploads video files from a folder on your computer to a YouTube channel you have connected, as private videos with a scheduled publish time.
  • Sets the title, description, tags, thumbnail, captions and playlist you prepared for each video.
  • Reads that channel's list of scheduled and published videos to choose the next free time slot and to confirm it is uploading to the right channel.
  • Keeps a small log on your computer so the same file is never uploaded twice.

It does nothing else. It does not delete videos, read comments, post to other services or access any other part of your Google account.
