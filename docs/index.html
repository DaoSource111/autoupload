AutoUpload uploads and schedules your videos on YouTube, from your own computer.

A free, native app by Ellipsio Studio for Windows, macOS and Linux. It sends your videos straight to the YouTube channel you connect, as private uploads with a publish date, communicates with no one but Google, and lets an AI agent do the busy work if you want.

The app

Put each finished video in its channel's queue folder with a short details file. Preview dates shows the publishing slot each one will get; Upload asks you to confirm, then sends them. You can try every button without a Google account: start the app with --demo.

Why it is different

  • Native, written in Rust. One small program per system, with nothing else to install: no Python, no Node, no browser engine inside.
  • Local-first. No AutoUpload server or account. Your files, sign-in and history stay on your computer, and it uses your own Google project and quota.
  • Built for AI agents. Claude Code, Codex and other agents can queue your videos with titles, descriptions and tags, and preview the dates. They upload only if you allow it, and the app shows everything they did.
  • Safe by default. Nothing is uploaded without your confirmation, nothing goes public at once, and the same file is never uploaded twice.

Let an AI agent do the busy work

AutoUpload works as an MCP server, the standard way AI agents use tools. Tell your agent “queue every video in my exports folder with a good title and tags, then preview the dates”, and watch it happen in the Activity tab. How to connect an agent.

What AutoUpload does

  • Uploads video files from a folder on your computer to a YouTube channel you have connected, as private videos with a scheduled publish time.
  • Sets the title, description, tags, thumbnail, captions and playlist you prepared for each video.
  • Reads that channel's list of scheduled and published videos to choose the next free time slot and to confirm it is uploading to the right channel.
  • Keeps a small log on your computer so the same file is never uploaded twice.

It does nothing else. It does not delete videos, read comments, post to other services or access any other part of your Google account.

What you authorize when you click “Allow”

The first time you connect a channel, Google shows its own consent screen listing the permissions below, in Google's words. Nothing happens until you choose Allow. Select a permission to see exactly how AutoUpload uses it.

“Manage your YouTube videos”https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.upload
What AutoUpload does with it
Uploads the video files you queued, as private videos with a scheduled publish time, and sets their thumbnails.
What it never does with it
It never uploads a file you did not queue, and it never uploads to a channel other than the one you connected.
“See, edit, and permanently delete your YouTube videos, ratings, comments and captions”https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.force-ssl
What AutoUpload does with it
Adds the caption files you supplied, adds the new video to the playlist you named, and updates the videos it uploaded itself (for example, their scheduled publish time).
What it never does with it
  • It never deletes a video.
  • It never rates videos, and never reads, posts or deletes comments.
  • It never edits videos it did not upload.
Why Google's wording is broader
Google groups captions, playlists and video updates under this single permission; there is no narrower one. The sentence describes everything the permission would allow, not what AutoUpload does.
“View your YouTube account”https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly
What AutoUpload does with it
Reads your channel's ID, to confirm it is uploading to the channel you connected, and the dates of your scheduled and published videos, to pick the next free time slot.
What it never does with it
It never reads your watch history, subscriptions, messages or analytics, and it stores nothing it reads except the channel ID.

Those three are the only permissions AutoUpload requests. It never asks for your email address, your name or profile, Gmail, Drive, contacts or any Google Cloud service, and it never sees your Google password.
